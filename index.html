The problem

One dataset can contain many kinds of anomalies.

An unusual value, a sudden change over time, a repeated payment, a shared address, a network cluster, and a copied passage are different patterns. Each requires a detector built for that kind of data.

Before any test runs, the system needs to understand the dataset, identify which detectors are compatible, and make their assumptions and known false positives visible.

Anomaly detectors

Run the right tests for the data in front of you.

A detector is a reusable test for one pattern: duplicate payments, unusual timing, shared entities, geographic clusters, copied text, and more.

Anomaly profiles the dataset, recommends up to ten compatible detectors, and shows why each was selected before you approve the run.

Findings and report

Every proposed finding points back to the data.

Anomaly turns detector signals into draft claims with evidence references, source and detector hashes, recorded runs, previews, and calculations.

Replay checks the math. A separate reviewer challenges the draft. You decide what becomes a finding.

01

Signal

A recorded pattern linked to its detector run.

02

Proposed finding

A draft claim linked to evidence and calculations.

03

Replay and review

Calculations and wording are checked independently.

04

Your decision

Accept, revise, reject, or leave the claim unresolved.

Traceable claims

Claims retain signal IDs, source and detector hashes, run IDs, evidence references, and replayable calculations.

Reviewed findings

Only claims accepted after replay and independent review enter the final findings file.

Report and charts

Anomaly writes a Markdown report, unresolved questions, and deterministic SVG charts from accepted findings.
